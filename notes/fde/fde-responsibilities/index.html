FDE Responsibilities

Follow one FDE through a real-world style project and learn the ten responsibilities that take a vague customer request all the way to a live solution with measurable results.

Meet the customer: ABC Insurance

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) works directly with the customer, close to their real problem, instead of sitting far away in a product team. The FDE listens, understands the customer’s technology, builds a solution, puts it live, and then checks that the customer really benefits. An FDE does not just write code; an FDE carries a customer’s problem all the way to a working solution that makes a real difference to the business.

One story, start to finishTo keep things simple, this whole note follows one example. ABC Insurance sells motor and health insurance. When a customer has an accident or a hospital bill, they file a claim. Think of it like a busy railway reservation counter in the days before online booking: every form is read and typed in by hand, and the queue keeps growing.

How ABC handles claims today:

500claims arrive every day
250claims employees
30 minof staff time per claim

Every claim comes with documents (claim form, hospital bill, police report, photos), mostly as PDF files. Employees open each PDF, read it, type the details by hand into ABC’s Claims Management System (CMS), then check everything again. Customers wait too long and are unhappy.

Meera, the Head of Claims, meets the FDE and says: “Our claims process is too slow. We need AI to make it faster.” This one sentence is where the FDE’s journey starts. The people you will meet:

PersonRole at ABC Insurance
MeeraHead of Claims. She owns the business problem and wants faster claims.
ArjunIT Manager. He knows ABC’s systems, servers and security rules.
AnitaA claims officer, one of the daily users of the new solution.
RahulA customer who filed a claim. His claim appears in our examples.

1 · Technical Discovery

Technical Discovery means understanding the customer’s existing technology before building a solution. The first question is not “what shall I code?” but “What does the customer’s technical world look like, and what must we work with?”

The family doctorBefore giving any medicine, a good doctor examines you, asks questions and checks your reports. An FDE does the same with the customer’s technology. Skip this step and the solution may not fit the customer’s systems, and a lot of work has to be redone later.

At ABC, the FDE does not open a code editor. The first days are spent with Arjun and his IT team.

Understand the existing technical environment

Where does your application run? Which database do you use? How do your systems talk to each other? Arjun explains: employees use a Claims Web App, which talks to a Backend API, which stores data in a PostgreSQL database. Everything runs on AWS, and staff log in through company single sign-on (SSO).

Why this matters: the new solution must fit into this setup, not fight it; it avoids surprises such as choosing a tool ABC is not allowed to use; and it shows the customer the FDE respects their existing work.

List the systems, applications, databases, APIs and infrastructure

A constraint is a limitation. A dependency is something the solution relies on to work.

If the Claims API is down, the AI service cannot save any claim details. The FDE must understand these relationships now and plan for them in the design.

Understand security, access and compliance

Insurance data is private. The FDE cannot simply connect an AI to all of ABC’s data, so asks: Who may see claim information? How do users log in? What data can the AI see? Where may customer data be stored?

ABC has a strict rule: “Customer documents must never leave our approved AWS environment.” And Anita should only see claims assigned to her. So the AI service must run inside ABC’s approved cloud, and users must be checked before they see anything:

Discovery is learning, not building. The better you understand the customer’s world, the fewer expensive surprises appear later.

2 · Problem Definition

Problem Definition is where the FDE works out what problem the customer is really trying to solve before deciding what to build. Customers usually describe a feeling (“it is slow”), not the cause. Discovery told us what ABC has; Problem Definition tells us what needs fixing. Without it, you can build a very good solution to the wrong problem.

Understand the actual business problem

The FDE investigates the human workflow, not only the software: How long does one claim take? How many arrive each day? What exact steps does an employee follow? After sitting with Anita’s team, the FDE breaks the 30 minutes down:

#Step done by the employeeTimeType of work
1Receive the claim2 minSimple
2Read the PDF documents8 minManual reading
3Pick out the needed details5 minManual work
4Type the details into the CMS10 minManual typing
5Check that everything is correct5 minVerification
Total30 min

Separate the symptom from the problem

What the customer saysThe business impactThe technical cause
“Our system is slow.”Claims take too long and customers wait.A manual document-review step waits for a human every single time.
Symptom: “system is slow”Impact: claims take too longCause: manual document review
Fixing the symptom If the FDE had just “made the system faster”, say with a bigger server, claims would still wait for humans to read PDFs. The real delay would remain.

Identify the root technical problem

The FDE walks the process step by step to find where the delay is created:

PDF uploadedEmployee reads PDFEmployee extracts detailsEmployee types into CMSEmployee verifies

Root technical problem: “There is no automated step in the claims workflow to read claim documents and pass their details to the CMS.”

Define requirements and constraints

A requirement is what the solution must do. A constraint is what it must respect or cannot do.

TypeABC Insurance
RequirementThe system must read claim documents and pick out key details automatically.
RequirementAn employee must still be able to check the details before they are final.
ConstraintCustomer documents must not be stored outside ABC’s approved cloud.
ConstraintThe solution must work with the existing Claims Management System.
ConstraintOnly authorised employees can see a claim.

Identify the expected outcomes

Meera says: “We want employees to spend less time reading documents.” The FDE turns that into a before-and-after picture:

BeforeAfter
Employee reads the documentAI reads the document
Employee picks out detailsAI picks out details
Employee types details into the CMSDetails are sent to the CMS automatically
Employee verifiesEmployee only checks and verifies

Expected outcomes: less manual work, faster claim processing, fewer typing mistakes, and more claims handled every day.

Write a clear technical problem statement

Before (vague)

“We need AI to make claims faster.”

After (clear)

“Employees today read PDF claim documents and type the details into the CMS by hand. We need an automated workflow that extracts the required claim details from the PDFs and sends them to the existing CMS, while following ABC’s security rules and keeping an employee in the loop for verification.”

A good problem statement says four things: what is happening now, who is affected, what is needed, and what limits apply.

3 · Solution Architecture

Solution Architecture means designing how all the pieces will work together to solve the problem. It is the blueprint, like a house plan drawn before construction starts. Discovery showed what ABC has; Problem Definition showed what must be solved.

Design the overall solution

The goal: read claim documents automatically, pick out the important details, and send them to the existing claim system. One key design choice: the AI does the reading and typing, but an employee still verifies the result. That keeps quality high and builds trust.

Identify the required components

4Validation ServiceChecks the extracted details (does this policy number exist?)
5Backend APIPasses the checked details to the CMS
6Claims Management SystemExisting system that stores the claim
7DatabaseStores the final claim record
8AuthenticationMakes sure only authorised users can use the system

Select technologies that fit

Choose technology because it fits the customer’s needs, not because it is popular.

DatabasePostgreSQLABC already uses it, so no new database is needed

Check the four qualities

Scalability

What happens when work grows? 500 claims a day today, maybe 5,000 next year, without a full rebuild.

Reliability

What happens when something fails? If the AI fails on one claim, don’t crash or lose it: retry later from a queue.

Security

Is customer data protected? Only authorised users and services can reach the documents.

Maintainability

Can another engineer understand it? Separate services (upload, AI, validation, integration), each with one clear job.

4 · Rapid Prototyping

Rapid Prototyping means building a small, quick test version to answer one question: “Can we actually make this work?” The architecture on paper looks good, but paper is not proof. Test the riskiest parts early, with real ABC data.

Customer problemQuick prototypeTest with real dataCustomer feedbackImproveProduction solution

Why prototype?

Many unknowns remain: Will the AI understand ABC’s documents? Will the details be accurate? Can we reach the Claims API? Is it fast enough? Will staff like this way of working?

Build a quick Proof of Concept

A Proof of Concept (PoC) is a small working experiment that proves an idea can work. It doesn’t need to look beautiful. ABC has thousands of old claim PDFs; the FDE picks just 20 samples of different types (motor and health).

5 · Production Engineering

Production Engineering turns a working prototype into a real, reliable system people use every day. A prototype proves the idea works; production engineering makes sure it keeps working in the real world. At ABC: 500 real claims a day, 250 employees, real customer data, and no falling over when something goes wrong.

PrototypeBetter architectureReal dataSecurityError handlingPerformanceMonitoringAutomated deploymentProduction

This move from PoC to production is usually where most of the engineering effort goes.

Write maintainable code

Prototype code is written fast, often all in one file. Production code must be clean, organised and easy for another engineer to understand.

prototype
app.js
  - API code
  - AI code
  - database code
  - login
  - error handling
production
claims-app/
|-- api/
|-- authentication/
|-- ai-service/
|-- database/
|-- configuration/
|-- error-handling/
In the field (FDE) Avoid code only you understand. If you are unavailable tomorrow, ABC’s own engineers must still be able to maintain it. You will leave; the system stays.

Error handling and logging

A prototype might just say “Error”. Production must handle problems politely and record what happened. If the Claims API is unavailable, the system keeps the claim in a queue, tells the user “The claim will be saved shortly”, and writes a log entry:

AI serviceClaims API unavailableError handlingLog the problemRetry later
2026-09-18 10:30:12  ERROR  Claims API request failed
Claim ID: C-88421   Reason: Timeout   Action: Queued for retry

Now an engineer can see what failed, when, and for which claim.

Testing and debugging

Before release, the FDE tests more than the happy path. Three kinds of tests are especially important:

Test typeExample at ABCExpected result
Normal caseAnita uploads a clear motor claim PDFCorrect details extracted and saved
Error caseThe Claims API is down during uploadSystem handles the error and retries
Security caseA user tries to open a claim not assigned to themAccess denied

Meera reports: “Sometimes the AI puts the wrong amount.” The FDE follows the data step by step:

DocumentText extractionAI readingValidationSaved amount

The mistake happens when a bill shows two amounts (total and paid). The FDE fixes that rule.

Performance and scalability

Performance = how fast the system works. Scalability = whether it can handle more users and more work. The FDE thinks about response time, users, requests, database load, AI delay and resource use.

100 claims/day500 claims/day5,000 claims/daymore

If 250 employees upload at the start of the day, the system must not slow down or fall over.

Security

Production uses real customer data. The FDE covers secure login and authorisation, protection of passwords, keys and other secrets, encryption of data at rest and in transit, and API security and access control.

EmployeeLoginAuthenticationAuthorisationClaims featureOnly allowed data

Configuration and environments

The app runs in development, testing and production, each with its own settings. Never hard-code these values.

SettingDevelopmentProduction
Databasedev-databaseproduction-database
Claims API addresstest Claims APIreal Claims API

With proper configuration, the same code runs everywhere without changes.

Monitoring and reliability

“It is deployed, my work is finished” is never true. Monitoring tells you what is happening (errors, response times, failed API calls, request counts). Reliability means the system keeps working even when one part has a problem. If the AI service suddenly takes 10 seconds per document, monitoring raises an alert before staff start complaining.

Documentation and handover

A useful note might say: “The application reads claim documents from the upload service. It runs on AWS. If the Claims API fails, check the API logs first.”

6 · Enterprise Integration

Enterprise Integration means connecting the solution to the customer’s existing systems so they exchange data correctly. Real companies already have applications, databases, APIs, login systems, cloud infrastructure and sometimes very old (legacy) software. The FDE almost never builds everything from scratch. At ABC, the new AI workflow works with the CMS, Policy System, payment system and SSO; it does not replace them.

Work with APIs

An API is a way for one software system to talk to another. The FDE must understand the endpoint (address), the request (what to send), the response, how to authenticate, what errors look like, and the rate limits. ABC’s Claims API creates a claim like this:

POST /claims · request body
{
  "customerName": "Rahul",
  "policyNumber": "P12345",
  "claimAmount": 50000,
  "accidentDate": "2026-09-10"
}

The API answers with a success message and a claim ID, or with an error the FDE must handle.

AI workflowPOST /claimsCMS creates claimClaim ID returnedShown to employee
Same date, two formats The extracted value “10/09/2026” becomes "2026-09-10" (ISO format, year-month-day) in the API request. Converting formats like this is everyday integration work.

Database integration

Sometimes the needed information sits in a database. Should we query it directly, or go through an existing service? It depends on ABC’s architecture and security rules. The safer choice is usually through a service, not straight to the database.

Authentication and authorisation

Authentication

“Who are you?” Anita logs in with her company account, and the system confirms her identity.

Authorisation

“What may you access?” She can open only claims assigned to her team, even though the AI could technically read every claim.

Common integration problems

8 · Evaluation

Test against known answers

The FDE takes 200 real claims that Anita’s team already processed by hand, runs them through the AI, and compares each field with the correct value staff typed earlier. This checks functionality (does it do what it should?) and performance (how fast and stable?).

9 · Customer Adoption

Customer Adoption means making sure the customer’s people actually start using the solution, and use it correctly. A working system nobody uses has no value. Some ABC staff are nervous (“What if the AI makes a mistake and I get blamed?”); others don’t know where to click. If they keep working the old way, ABC gains nothing.

Help people start

Tool readyStaff unsureDemo + short trainingStaff start using it
  • Run a short live demo with real claims.
  • Start with a friendly pilot group (Anita’s team) who become champions and help colleagues.
  • Say clearly that the employee still verifies every claim. The AI is a helper, not a replacement.

10 · Business Value

Business Value means showing that the solution truly matters to the customer’s business, not only that it works technically. It answers the leader’s question: “So what did we gain?” Meera doesn’t care about extraction models or APIs; she cares about customers getting claims settled faster and her team coping without stress.

The stages are not always strictly one after another, but a real project usually moves in this order. The weeks are illustrative.

Why the stages overlap

  • Prototype → Problem Definition. Anita asks for the hospital name: a new requirement, so the problem statement is updated.
  • Evaluation → Production Engineering. The 12-point accuracy gap sends the FDE back to improve how scanned forms are read.
  • Adoption → Architecture. Users ask to highlight uncertain fields, a small design change.

Because the FDE sits with the customer every day, these changes are noticed early and fixed cheaply, not months later.

Key ideas to remember

Understand before you build

Discovery and Problem Definition come first.

Design, then test small

Architecture and prototypes reduce risk.

Make it real

Production, integration and deployment turn an idea into a working system.

Prove it

Evaluation, adoption and business value show the work was worth it.

About the numbers 500 claims a day, 100 requests per minute, 90% accuracy, 30 → 8 minutes per claim and the 12-week plan are illustrative examples for learning, not real data.

Key terms

Technical Discovery
Understanding a customer’s existing systems, data flows, constraints and security rules before designing or building anything.
Problem Definition
Turning a vague customer complaint into a clear statement of the real problem, its root cause, requirements, constraints and expected outcomes.
Root cause
The actual reason behind a problem, as opposed to the symptom the customer notices (e.g. manual PDF reading, not “the system is slow”).
Requirement vs constraint
A requirement is what the solution must do; a constraint is a limit it must respect, such as “documents must stay in the approved cloud”.
Solution Architecture
The blueprint of a solution: its components, how data flows between them, and the technologies chosen to fit the customer.
Technical feasibility
Whether a proposed technology can really work in the customer’s actual environment, usually checked with a prototype.

Optical Character Recognition: converting text in an image, such as a scanned bill, into machine-readable text.
Production Engineering
Turning a working prototype into a reliable everyday system with clean code, error handling, testing, security, configuration and monitoring.
Rate limit
The maximum number of requests an API accepts in a period of time, e.g. 100 requests per minute.
Enterprise Integration
Connecting a new solution to a customer’s existing systems so they exchange data safely, correctly and reliably.
Authentication vs authorisation
Authentication answers “who are you?”; authorisation answers “what are you allowed to access?”.
Rollback plan
A prepared way to quickly return to the previous working version if a new release causes serious problems.
Success criteria
Measurable targets agreed with the customer in advance, such as “at least 90% field accuracy”, used to judge whether a solution is good.
Customer adoption
Getting the customer’s people to actually use the solution correctly, through demos, training, guides and support.
