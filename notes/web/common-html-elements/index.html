Common HTML Elements

Fill a page with real content: headings at the right level, paragraphs, line breaks, and the formatting tags that make words bold, italic, highlighted, crossed out, raised or lowered.

Heading tags: <h1> to <h6>

The newspaperOpen any newspaper: a huge headline on top, smaller section titles below it, and even smaller sub-titles over each story. You can scan the page in seconds. Headings are the titles and subtitles of a web page; they show what each part is about.

Look at any Wikipedia article: the article name is the biggest title, then section names, then smaller sub-sections inside them. HTML gives you six heading levels for exactly this:

<h1> biggest · main title<h2> sub-title<h3><h4><h5><h6> smallest

Built-in spacing Browsers automatically add some white space (a margin) before and after every heading, so you don’t need empty lines around them.

Why heading tags matter

Headings are not just “big text”. They help both people and machines understand your page.

1 · Structure: clear organisation

Headings divide content into small, clear parts, just like a book:

<h1>My Website</h1>
<h2>About Me</h2>
<h3>My Hobbies</h3>

2 · SEO

Search Engine Optimisation. Google reads your headings to learn what the page is about, so clear headings help it show your page to the right people.

3 · Accessibility

People using screen readers (tools that read text aloud) can jump from heading to heading to find what they need.

Accessibility means… making your website usable by everyone, including people with disabilities. Good habit: one <h1> per page, and don’t skip levels (go h2 → h3, not h2 → h5).
Don’t pick a heading for its size If you just want bigger text, that is a job for CSS. Choose the heading level by its place in the outline.

The paragraph tag <p>

The paragraph tag holds normal text, just like a paragraph in your notebook. Everything between <p> and </p> becomes one paragraph.

<p>This is my first paragraph in HTML.</p>

Opening tag · a new paragraph starts

Content · the paragraph’s text

Closing tag · the paragraph ends

inside <body>
<body>
    <p>This is my first paragraph in HTML.</p>
    <p>HTML helps to display text clearly on a web page.</p>
</body>
Spaces and Enter are ignored Inside HTML, pressing Enter or typing many spaces in your code does not create new lines on the page. The browser collapses them into a single space. That is why we need <p> and <br>.

Line break <br>

The <br> tag creates a line break: whatever follows it starts on a new line, inside the same paragraph.

br example
<h2>Using the br tag</h2>
<p>
  My name is Abhi.<br>
  I am learning HTML.<br>
  I love creating web pages.
</p>
A void element <br> has no closing tag (you may also see it written <br />). Use it for line breaks that belong to the content, like addresses or poems, not for adding space between blocks.

Formatting tags: making words stand out

Exam notesWhen you make notes before an exam, you bold key terms, slant a book title, run a highlighter over the formula you must remember, and cross out a wrong answer. HTML formatting tags do the same to text on a web page.

Some formatting tags only change how text looks. Others also tell the browser, search engines and screen readers what the text means (for example, “this is important”). That difference is the most important idea in this section.

Bold: <b> and <strong>

<b> · bold for style

<b> makes text bold. It catches the eye but adds no extra meaning; it only changes the appearance.

<p>This is a <b>bold</b> word.</p>
This is a bold word.

<strong> · bold because it is important

<strong> also displays bold, but it tells browsers, search engines and screen readers that the text is important, serious or urgent.

<p><strong>Warning:</strong> Don't touch the hot surface!</p>
Warning: Don't touch the hot surface!

On a safety notice, “Warning” is not bold for decoration. It is bold because it matters. That is <strong>.

Remember (VIP) <b> just makes text look bold. <strong> makes it bold and says “this part is important!”

Italic: <i> and <em>

<i> · italic for style

<i> makes text italic, slanting slightly to the right. Use it to set a word apart in style: book titles, foreign words, special terms.

<p>This is an <i>italicized</i> word.</p>
This is an italicized word.

<em> · italic because it is stressed

<em> (emphasis) also shows italic, but it tells the browser: “Hey, stress this word. Give it special attention!” Screen readers can change their voice for it.

<p>I said I wanted <em>coffee</em>, not tea!</p>
I said I wanted coffee, not tea!

Highlight and fine print: <mark> and <small>

<mark> · highlighter pen

<mark> highlights text, usually with a yellow background, to draw attention to relevant information, such as search terms or key findings in a document.

<p>This is <mark>highlighted</mark> text.</p>

<small> · the small print

<small> makes text smaller than the normal text. It is like a small note under the main text: a disclaimer, copyright line or condition.

<p>Free delivery on all orders. <small>Conditions apply.</small></p>
<p><small>© 2026 My Website</small></p>
Neither changes importance <mark> draws the eye (often to “what is relevant right now”), and <small> marks side comments and fine print. If you mean “important”, use <strong>.

Edits and outdated text: <del>, <ins>, <s>

The sabzi-wala’s boardWhen tomato prices drop, the vendor crosses out the old price on his board and writes the new one beside it. Strike-through says “no longer valid”; underline says “newly added”.

<del> · deleted

Text that was removed or replaced. Shown with a line through it, like crossing out a word in your notebook.

<ins> · inserted

Text that was added. Usually shown underlined to flag new or updated content.

<s> · no longer true

Text that is outdated or no longer accurate, but not a document edit. Also shown with a line through.

del and ins together
<p>The old price was <del>$100</del> now it's <ins>$80</ins>.</p>
s for outdated info
<p>The old price was <s>$50</s> now it's $30.</p>
<del> or <s>? They look the same. Use <del> when you actually removed or replaced something in the document. Use <s> when something is simply no longer true, like an old price or outdated info.

Below and above the line: <sub> and <sup>

<sub> · subscript

Small text written slightly below the normal line. Chemical formulas like H₂O, or math like x₂.

<sup> · superscript

Small text written slightly above the normal line. Powers like x², dates like 1st, footnote numbers.

sub
<p>Water formula is H<sub>2</sub>O.</p>
sup
<p>The area of a square is a<sup>2</sup>.</p>
<p>E = mc<sup>2</sup></p>

The obsolete <big> tag

<big> was part of older HTML versions. It made the enclosed text one size larger than the text around it.

<p>This is a <big>bigger</big> word.</p>
Don’t use it <big> is obsolete in HTML5. Browsers still display it, but it is not valid modern HTML. To change size, use CSS (for example font-size), which you’ll learn later.
In the field (FDE) Customer apps often show AI output, search results or price changes. The semantic tags are the clean way to do it: <mark> for matched search terms, <del>/<ins> for a “what changed” diff view, <strong> for warnings. Screen readers, search engines and enterprise accessibility audits all read that meaning.

Key terms

Heading tags
The six elements <h1> to <h6> for titles and subtitles; h1 is the most important and largest, h6 the least.
SEO
Search Engine Optimisation: making a page easy for search engines like Google to understand, so it reaches the right searchers. Clear headings help.
Accessibility
Making a website usable by everyone, including people with disabilities who use tools such as screen readers.
Screen reader
Software that reads a web page aloud; users can jump between headings and hear emphasis from tags like <strong> and <em>.
Paragraph (<p>)
A block element for normal text; the browser adds space before and after each one.
Line break (<br>)
A void element that moves the following content onto a new line within the same block, without a paragraph gap.
Formatting tags
Inline tags that change how text looks, and in some cases what it means, such as bold, italic, highlighted or struck-through text.
Semantic tag
A tag that carries meaning, not just style, for example <strong> (important) or <em> (stressed), compared with <b> and <i>.
<del> vs <s>
Both draw a line through text; <del> marks content removed from the document, <s> marks content that is no longer accurate.
Subscript / superscript
Small text placed below (<sub>) or above (<sup>) the normal line, as in H₂O or x².
Obsolete tag
A tag from older HTML that is no longer valid in HTML5, such as <big>; use CSS instead.

Self-check

Pick an answer. You’ll see right away whether it’s correct and why.

How many heading levels does HTML have?

  1. 3
  2. 6
  3. 8
  4. As many as you like

<h1> through <h6>: six levels, from most to least important.

You want slightly bigger text for a note that is not a section title. What should you do?

  1. Wrap it in <h3>
  2. Wrap it in <h1>
  3. Use <big>
  4. Keep it in <p> and change the size with CSS

Headings describe the outline of the page, not font size, and <big> is obsolete. Size is a CSS job.

Which tag says “this text is important”, not just “make it bold”?

  1. <strong>
  2. <b>
  3. <mark>
  4. <i>

<strong> looks bold and also carries the meaning “important” for screen readers and search engines.

You write three lines of an address in one <p>, pressing Enter after each line in your code. How does the browser show it?

  1. Three lines
  2. Three paragraphs
  3. One line, with the line breaks collapsed into spaces
  4. An error

HTML collapses Enter and extra spaces into a single space. Add <br> where each new line should start.

Which line correctly shows the chemical formula for water?

  1. H<sup>2</sup>O
  2. H<sub>2</sub>O
  3. H<small>2</small>O
  4. H<br>2O

The 2 in H₂O sits below the line, so it is subscript: <sub>.

A shop page shows a price that was changed from ₹500 to ₹450. Which pair fits best?

  1. <mark> and <small>
  2. <b> and <i>
  3. <sub> and <sup>
  4. <del> for ₹500 and <ins> for ₹450

<del> marks removed content (struck through) and <ins> marks inserted content (underlined).

What is the difference between <i> and <em>?

  1. <em> is bold, <i> is italic
  2. None at all
  3. Both look italic, but <em> also marks the word as stressed
  4. <i> is obsolete

They look the same by default; <em> adds meaning that screen readers can voice.

Why do headings help people who use screen readers?

  1. They can jump from heading to heading to find a section
  2. Headings are read louder
  3. Screen readers skip everything except headings
  4. Headings change the page colour

Screen readers list the headings so users can navigate a page by its outline instead of listening to everything.

All of it, in one sentence

Use h1–h6 to build the page’s outline, <p> for text and <br> for line breaks, and choose formatting tags by meaning: strong/em for importance and stress, b/i for style, plus mark, small, del, ins, s, sub and sup for their special jobs.
